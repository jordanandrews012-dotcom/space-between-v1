You are building a wave, not a broadcast. The earlier sends establish receipt at lower levels of government, which gives the higher-level sends a context to be triaged into. The last send (Ford) arrives at the top of an already-warm ladder.

Policy Submission — The Space Between: A Frontline Series on Ontario Hospital Safety and the Case for a Healthcare Special Constable Designation

From: Jordan Andrews · jordan.andrews@[your-email]
Date: April 2026

[[ OPENER — insert the personalised opener for this recipient from the list below ]]

I am a Protection Services Officer at Queensway Carleton Hospital in Ottawa. I have spent a decade in security, the past four years specifically in hospital protection services. The attached three-paper policy series was authored in response to a specific incident at my workplace in April 2026, in which a woman in acute psychiatric crisis, with a knife disclosed in her vehicle, waited approximately ninety minutes for police to respond. Section 17 of the Mental Health Act was not invoked. The incident escalated. She was arrested, not assessed. She left the hospital in a police vehicle rather than in clinical care.

That incident is not isolated. It is a specific instance of a structural authority gap in Ontario hospitals — a gap that becomes statistically visible when you look at the Canadian hospital violence record of the past sixteen months. Thompson chapel shotgun, December 2024. London Victoria ER shooting, December 2024. Halifax Infirmary stabbings, January 2025. Vancouver General nurse strangulation, April 2025. Brandon ED nurse choking, April 2025. Winnipeg Health Sciences Centre sexual assault spree, July 2025. Saskatoon St. Paul's sawed-off shotgun and knives in the emergency department, November 2025. Edmonton Royal Alexandra Hospital ED stabbing, April 2026 — two weeks ago, as of this writing.

The three documents attached to this email are the formal policy submission responding to this pattern. Together, they propose the designation of trained hospital Protection Services Officers as Healthcare Special Constables under Ontario's Community Safety and Policing Act, Part XIV — the same mechanism under which Ottawa's OC Transpo Special Constable Unit already operates, administered by the Ottawa Police Services Board with Solicitor General approval.

Paper I — Hold the Line (19-page editorial): The mass-casualty risk case, written at the register of a Globe and Mail op-ed. Opens with the Canadian 2024–2026 hospital violence timeline; establishes the structural claim that a hospital mass casualty event is now a matter of when, not whether; addresses the workforce positioned to prevent it.

Paper II — Protecting the Protectors (27-page frontline case): The bridge. Identity, evidence base, retention problem, fiscal context anchored to the Financial Accountability Office's October 2025 Health Sector Spending Plan Review, Alberta proof-of-concept, training framework mapped to Ontario Police College standards, action framework, bipartisan political case, and a direct address to the workforce this is written about.

Paper III — The Space Between (54-page policy specification): The full specification. Three-stage failure model, complete legislative authority map, Charter analysis under sections 7, 9, and 15, clinical primacy enforcement framework with eight specific operational rules, Ottawa two-site pilot design with binding evaluation criteria, pilot containment framework with automatic off-ramp conditions, full fiscal analysis anchored to police time reallocation as the primary offset, and forty-two references from peer-reviewed, government, and institutional sources.

Every claim in the series is anchored to publicly available, peer-reviewed, or institutionally published sources. The fiscal figures are traceable to the Financial Accountability Office. The case law is traceable to CanLII. The Canadian incident record is traceable to mainstream news reporting and provincial nurses' union publications. The clinical data is traceable to CIHI, Kurdyak et al. (JAMA Network Open, 2021), Fung & Kim (European Psychiatry, 2024), and the Canadian Federation of Nurses Unions' 2025 report.

I am not writing as an expert. I am writing as a frontline worker who has done this job for a decade and who has documented the gap carefully enough that the documentation is now submittable. I do not expect to be the person who writes the eventual legislation. I expect to be the person who made the case for why it needs to be written.

// Backup if a Recipient's Server Blocks HTML

Some government email servers reject .html attachments as a security policy. If an email bounces, try:

Option A: Zip the three HTML files into a single space_between_html.zip and attach that. Most servers allow zip.

Option B: Upload the three HTML files to Google Drive or Dropbox, make the links shareable, and include the links in the email body. The PDFs still attach directly.

Option C: Send only the three PDFs (which are accepted universally) and note in the email that the HTML versions are available on request.
